# Concepts

A concept is the one idea the whole film is built on. Pick the one that is most convincing when *seen*, not the easiest to build, and name it in STYLE.md. Each line says how it is built in Motion Kit.

## Twelve concepts

1. **The number.** One figure carries the film: it counts, it is compared, it is explained. `counter` + `ring`, then `chart`, then the line that gives it meaning. Best for results, growth, performance.
2. **Before / after.** The problem in its world, then the same frame transformed. Two scenes with the same layout and camera, a `wipe` between them, the palette changing at the wipe.
3. **Inside the product.** The camera travels across one interface: a `device` that scrolls, pushes into a detail, a `zoom` into the next view. Titles stay short and to one side. For apps and tools.
4. **The word.** Kinetic typography: a sentence built one strong word per scene, `chars` and `mask` reveals, `push` and `whip` between them, a beat per word. For manifestos, launches, intros.
5. **The journey.** One thing moves through stages: the same object (`image`, `logo`, `html` path) in every scene, each scene a step, `push` in one direction throughout. For processes and "how it works".
6. **The build.** Something assembles: `code` typed, then the result appears in a `device`, then the number it achieves. For developer tools.
7. **Three truths.** Three short claims, each its own scene with its own visual (never three tiles), then the one that ties them. `cards` only as a recap.
8. **The map.** A field of points (`particles`, an `html` SVG map) that a line connects; for networks, reach, community.
9. **The question.** Open on a question the audience has, answer it visually, end on the brand. Good for explainers and ads.
10. **The countdown.** Time or a sequence drives the cuts: 3, 2, 1, or step 1–4, each a beat. For launches and events.
11. **The reveal.** Withhold the subject: detail, detail, detail, then pull back (camera `from` zoom 1.6 to 1) to show the whole. For hardware, design, brands.
12. **The contrast.** Two forces, two colours, two sides of the frame; they meet in the end. For comparisons and positioning.

## Openers (the first 2 seconds)
A single word that lands with `chars`. A counter already moving. A push through a texture into the first frame. A question. A detail of the product, unexplained. Never a logo first, unless it is a logo sting.

## Closers
The logo with `shine` on a light mesh; the address as a `label` with `pill: false`; one line of promise above it. Hold the last frame alive (camera still pushing) for 1–1.5 s.

## Explainers (30–90 s)
Problem (one scene) → idea (one scene) → how it works (three to five scenes, one step each, the same visual world) → the result (a number) → the call to act. A scene per voiceover sentence; everything named in the voice appears on screen in the same second. Put the script in vo-script.md.

## Presenting features without tiles
- One feature per scene, each with its own visual, the camera moving from one to the next.
- A single `device` with the camera pushing to the part each feature lives in.
- A `cards` stack that fans, each card lit in turn with `cycle`, only to sum up.
- A chart that grows once per feature.
